**When to use it:** Planning a household's dinners for the coming week. It checks the calendar to see how many dinners are needed, reviews saved recipes and recent plans, records a proposed plan, and sends it for feedback. Shopping is a separate step.

**1. Gather context**

- Recall preferences from memory: dinners per week, max cook time, dietary preferences (low sodium and low sugar), favorite creators, recurring breakfasts and staples, and feedback from past weeks.
- Read the recipe database: name, source, type, protein, total time, rating, last made, and notes.
- Read the last 3-4 weekly meal plans so dinners don't repeat too often.

**2. Check the calendar to decide how many dinners**

- Look at the target week plus the weekends on either side. Watch for travel, flights, hotels, out-of-town blocks, dinners out, parties, date nights, evening events (about 5-8 PM), houseguests, and nights one of you is away.
- Away the whole week: plan no dinners, still create the week's record with a note, and ask whether you want anything for before or after the trip.
- Leaving partway through the week: plan only the nights before you go (often 1 dinner), and avoid recipes that leave a lot of perishable leftovers.
- Coming back partway through the week: plan the nights after you're home, starting with something quick for the first night.
- Busy week: lean on fast fallback meals.
- Guests: note it and consider scaling up servings.
- Otherwise use the usual 2-3 dinners, never more than the number of free nights at home.
- If the calendar can't be read, plan the usual count and say so.
- Briefly say what I saw, like "You fly out Wed, so I planned 1 dinner for Monday."

**3. Choose the dinners**

- Stick to the cook-time limit and dietary preferences.
- Favor top-rated recipes, use rarely made ones only now and then, and skip anything rated "Not again".
- Avoid anything made in the last 2 weeks or so, and vary the proteins.
- Consider the season, like chili and soups in fall and winter.
- Skip placeholder recipes that are missing ingredients, and say what's missing.
- Pick the dinners plus 1-2 alternates. Optionally suggest one new idea from a creator you follow, clearly marked as new and taken from a real post.
- Assign each dinner to one of the free nights.

**4. Record the proposal**

- Create the week's entry in Meal Plans: "Week of <Monday date>", the week's start date, Status Proposed, the recipes linked, and notes on what the calendar showed.

**5. Propose it**

- Message you with what I saw on the calendar, how many dinners that means, and each dinner's night, cook time, and a one-line reason, plus the alternates and the Notion link.
- Ask for swaps. Once you approve, I'll build the grocery cart for your OK.
- Update the plan with your feedback, and mark it Approved when you sign off.
